How to Use the Garden Soil & Material Calculator

Choose the type of material you want to calculate. For a raised garden bed or garden bed, enter the length, width and soil depth. The calculator estimates the volume of soil required and converts it into cubic feet, cubic yards and liters.

For potting soil or potting mix, enter the approximate dimensions of your container and the number of containers. For compost, enter the area dimensions and the depth of compost you want to apply.

You can also use the soil mix calculator to divide a total volume into soil, compost and peat moss according to your chosen percentages.

Raised Bed Soil Calculator

A raised bed soil calculator helps you determine how much growing medium is needed to fill a raised garden bed. Enter the inside length and width of the bed along with the desired soil depth.

If you have multiple beds with the same dimensions, enter the number of beds. The calculator multiplies the volume of one bed by the number of beds.

Garden Bed Soil Calculator

A garden bed soil calculator can be used for both raised and ground-level beds. The basic calculation uses the area of the bed multiplied by the depth of material required.

Soil volume:

Length × Width × Depth = Soil Volume

For rectangular beds, all three measurements should use the same unit before calculating volume.

Potting Soil & Potting Mix Calculator

A potting soil calculator estimates how much potting mix is required to fill containers. Container shapes are often irregular or tapered, so the result should be treated as an estimate rather than an exact product quantity.

For multiple containers, the calculator multiplies the estimated volume of one container by the number of containers.

Compost Calculator

A compost calculator helps estimate the amount of compost needed for a garden bed or raised bed. The amount depends on the surface area and the depth of compost you want to add.

For example, a shallow compost layer requires much less material than filling an entire raised bed with compost.

Compost Calculator for Raised Beds

If you are adding compost to an existing raised garden bed, measure the inside length and width of the bed and decide how deep the compost layer should be. Enter those values in the compost calculator.

Peat Moss Calculator

A peat moss calculator can be used when peat moss is one component of a custom growing mix. Enter the total mix volume and the percentage of peat moss you want to use.

The calculator then estimates the peat moss volume and the number of bags based on the selected bag size.

Landscape Rock Calculator

A landscape rock calculator estimates the amount of rock needed to cover a defined area at a chosen depth. It can be useful for decorative rock, garden paths, borders and other landscaping projects.

Landscape rock volume:

Length × Width × Rock Depth = Rock Volume

The result is converted into cubic feet and cubic yards so you can compare it with landscaping material sold by the bag or by the cubic yard.

How Much Soil Do I Need for a Raised Garden Bed?

The amount of soil depends on the inside length, inside width and soil depth of the raised bed. A deeper bed requires more soil than a shallow bed with the same surface area.

If the bed has an irregular shape, divide it into simpler rectangular sections and calculate each section separately before adding the volumes together.

Should I Buy Extra Soil?

It is often practical to allow a small amount of extra material because soil and compost can settle after installation. Product bags can also vary slightly in their stated volume.

For a precise project, use the calculator result as the base quantity and compare it with the manufacturer's stated bag volume or bulk-material specifications.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much soil do I need for a raised bed?

Enter the inside length, width and soil depth of your raised bed. The calculator estimates the total soil volume and gives the result in cubic feet, cubic yards and liters.

What is a raised bed soil calculator?

A raised bed soil calculator estimates the volume of growing material required to fill a raised garden bed based on its dimensions and desired soil depth.

Can I use this as a raised garden bed soil calculator?

Yes. Enter the inside dimensions of your raised garden bed and the desired depth. You can also enter multiple beds if they have the same dimensions.

Can I use this as a raised bed dirt calculator?

Yes. Soil and dirt volume can be estimated from the same basic length, width and depth measurements. For gardening, however, the quality and composition of the material are also important.

Can I calculate garden bed soil?

Yes. Use the raised bed soil mode for rectangular beds or the compost mode when you only need to calculate a specific layer of material.

How does the potting soil calculator work?

The calculator estimates container volume from length, width and depth, then multiplies the result by the number of containers.

What is the difference between potting soil and potting mix?

The terms are often used interchangeably in gardening. Products can contain different combinations of organic matter, mineral material, peat, coconut coir, perlite, vermiculite and other ingredients, so always check the product label.

How do I calculate compost for a raised bed?

Measure the bed length and width and choose the compost depth you want to add. Multiply the bed area by the compost depth to estimate the compost volume.

How do I calculate peat moss?

Enter the total volume of your growing mix and the percentage of peat moss you want. The calculator determines the approximate peat moss volume.

How do I calculate rock for landscaping?

Measure the length and width of the area and decide how deep you want the rock layer. The calculator multiplies those measurements and converts the result to cubic feet and cubic yards.

How many bags of soil do I need?

Enter your project dimensions and select the bag size that matches the product you intend to buy. The calculator estimates the number of bags by dividing the required volume by the volume of one bag.

Does this calculator account for soil settling?

The calculated volume represents the geometric volume of the project. Soil, compost and other organic materials can settle over time, so the actual amount purchased may need to be slightly higher depending on the material and project.
