How to Calculate Soil for a Raised Garden Bed

To calculate how much soil a raised garden bed needs, multiply the bed's length by its width and then multiply the result by the soil depth.

Soil Volume = Length × Width × Soil Depth

When all three measurements are in feet, the result is cubic feet. To convert cubic feet to cubic yards, divide the cubic-foot result by 27.

Cubic Yards = Cubic Feet ÷ 27

How Much Soil Does a 4 × 8 Raised Bed Need?

A 4 × 8 raised bed is a common size for vegetable gardening. If the bed is filled to a depth of 12 inches (1 foot), it requires 32 cubic feet of soil before accounting for settling.

If you add a 10% settling allowance, the amount increases to about 35.2 cubic feet. The exact amount will depend on the depth you choose and how much the soil settles.

How Deep Should a Raised Garden Bed Be?

The ideal soil depth depends on what you plan to grow. Many common vegetables can grow well with around 12 inches of soil, while crops with deeper root systems may benefit from deeper beds.

If your raised bed is placed directly on the ground, plant roots may also be able to grow into the soil underneath the bed. A raised bed with a solid bottom generally needs enough soil depth to accommodate the plants' root systems within the bed itself.

What Soil Should You Use in a Raised Bed?

Raised garden beds are commonly filled with a mixture of quality topsoil and organic matter such as compost. Some gardeners also use materials such as peat moss or coco coir to help influence moisture retention and soil structure.

The best mixture depends on the plants you are growing, your existing soil, drainage conditions, climate, and the materials available to you.

Should You Add Extra Soil for Settling?

Yes. Soil can settle after it is added to a raised bed, particularly when organic materials break down or air spaces between particles collapse. Adding approximately 10% extra is a practical allowance for many raised-bed projects.

You can also keep some extra soil or compost available to top up the bed later in the growing season.
