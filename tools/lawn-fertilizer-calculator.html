How to Use the Lawn Fertilizer Calculator

Start by entering the length and width of your lawn. You can use feet or meters, and the calculator converts the measurements automatically.

Next, enter the fertilizer application rate shown on your fertilizer product or recommended for your lawn. The calculator uses this rate to determine the total fertilizer required for the lawn area.

If you know the fertilizer's N-P-K analysis, enter those values as well. This allows the calculator to estimate how much nitrogen is actually being applied with the calculated fertilizer amount.

You can also enter the fertilizer bag size to estimate how many bags you need to cover the calculated lawn area.

Understanding Fertilizer N-P-K Numbers

Fertilizer labels commonly display three numbers, such as 20-5-10. These numbers represent the fertilizer's nitrogen, phosphate, and potash percentages by weight.

N-P-K Meaning
First number Nitrogen (N)
Second number Phosphate (P₂O₅)
Third number Potash (K₂O)

Why Lawn Area Matters When Applying Fertilizer

Fertilizer recommendations are generally based on the size of the area being treated. Applying the same amount of fertilizer to a small lawn and a large lawn can result in very different application rates.

Measuring the lawn first helps you determine how much fertilizer is required while reducing the risk of purchasing too little or applying an excessive amount.

Using Fertilizer Bags

If you purchase fertilizer in bags, enter the weight of one bag into the calculator. The calculator will estimate how many bags are needed based on the total fertilizer requirement.

Because fertilizer bags are normally sold as whole bags, you may need to purchase the next whole bag above the calculated requirement.

Frequently Asked Questions About Lawn Fertilizer

How much fertilizer does my lawn need?

The amount depends on the size of your lawn and the recommended application rate of the fertilizer. Enter those values into the calculator to estimate the total fertilizer required.

What does N-P-K mean on fertilizer?

N-P-K represents nitrogen, phosphate, and potash. The numbers on a fertilizer label indicate the percentage of each component by weight.

Can I calculate the amount of nitrogen applied to my lawn?

Yes. Enter the nitrogen percentage from your fertilizer label. The calculator uses the total fertilizer amount to estimate the amount of nitrogen being applied.

Can I calculate fertilizer for multiple lawn sections?

Yes. If several lawn sections have identical dimensions, enter the number of identical areas and the calculator will include all of them in the total lawn area.

How do I know what fertilizer application rate to use?

Check the fertilizer product label for its recommended application rate. Recommendations can vary by fertilizer formulation, grass type, season, and lawn conditions.
